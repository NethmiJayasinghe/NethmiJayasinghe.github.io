Cerebellum-Inspired Memtransistors Enable Emergent Differentiation for Hardware-Efficient Novelty Detection

TL;DR

Asymmetric-contact-gated MoS₂ memtransistors emulate the cerebellum's emergent synaptic differentiation to rapidly flag novel events, detecting cardiac arrhythmias within a single heartbeat using 10,000-fold fewer operations than existing silicon-based approaches.

Abstract

AI today runs on silicon hardware, driving up the energy demand of data centers, while edge AI for healthcare, robotics, and autonomous vehicles faces even tighter power and latency budgets that incumbent architectures cannot meet. Biological neuronal networks point to alternative computing strategies - memory-logic colocation, asynchronous parallelism, spike-triggered computation - with vastly superior energy efficiency.

Drawing inspiration from the cerebellum, we demonstrate asymmetric-contact-gated MoS₂ memtransistors exhibiting bias-polarity-dependent excitatory/inhibitory short-term plasticity. Arrays of these devices exploit the evolving interplay between excitatory and inhibitory responses to emulate the cerebellum's emergent synaptic differentiation, enabling rapid identification of novel events. Applied to electrocardiogram data, arrhythmias are detected within a single heartbeat with 10,000-fold fewer operations than existing silicon-based approaches - a path toward computationally efficient, high-speed novelty detection for edge intelligence.
